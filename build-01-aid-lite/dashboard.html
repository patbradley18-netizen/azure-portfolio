Algorithmic Influence Detector, stages 1 to 4

Coordinated messaging in 84,270 English tweets from accounts Twitter attributed to the Internet Research Agency (FiveThirtyEight / Clemson release, file 1 of 13). Stage 1 computed locally; stage 2 re-runs the template detection every Monday on an Azure Function with no person involved, and tags the top messages with key phrases from Azure AI Language. Stage 3 adds who amplified whom and when the blasts happened. Stage 4 tests whether the themes reached the Congressional Record, against a control; they did not.

Who amplified whom

Stage 3, signal C. Every retweet is an arrow from the account that retweeted to the account retweeted. External arrows point at handles outside the dataset (parsed from "RT handle:"). Internal arrows point at another dataset account whose earlier original has the same body text. Being retweeted by these accounts says nothing about the retweeted account; it only shows what the operation chose to boost.

When: one-day blasts

Stage 3, signal D. The planned burst score (peak day over median day) was useless here, because 74.2% of templates were posted on a single day: median equals peak. The finding is the shape itself. Anchor phrase: one-day blast.

Did the themes reach the floor of Congress? No measurable trace.

Stage 4. Every speech in the Congressional Record from August 2017 to December 2018 (187,829 chunks of ~200 words, public domain) was scored for similarity to the 1,642 troll templates, and, as a control, to 1,642 random non-template tweets from the same accounts. Anchor phrase: politics resembles politics. Without the control this would have looked like a finding.

0.386average similarity to the troll templates
0.397average similarity to the random control (higher)
0.96%of chunks resemble a template (≥ 0.6)
+0.35 ptslift over the control. Democrats +0.66, Republicans +0.32

By month. The two lines never separate; no month shows a lift above 1.3 points.

What the "matches" actually are: shared topics

Templates most often scored as the nearest neighbour of a speech chunk. Every one is a news topic Congress was debating anyway.
