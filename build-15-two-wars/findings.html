Two wars, matched years: Vietnam and GWOT veterans at the same distance from service

The same survey, 28 times across 40 years, read on one clock: years since the veteran last left active duty. Vietnam veterans who served in-theater (1985–2021) against Iraq/Afghanistan veterans (2010–2025), ages 18–64, weighted; non-veterans surveyed in the same years are the baseline. Anchor phrase: same clock, different army. The honest window where both cohorts sit on the same clock is 16–22 years out.

Vietnam, in-theater (VVIETNAM)GWOT, Iraq/Afghanistan (VIRAQ/VAFGHAN)— — civilian reference

Work

GWOT in-theater veterans 16–22 years after separation had an unemployment rate of 2.4% (±0.9%) against 5.3% (±0.5%) for Vietnam in-theater veterans at the same distance — but those are different economies (2010–2025 vs 1991–2019). Against non-veterans surveyed in the same years, the GWOT cohort sat at 0.53× the civilian rate and the Vietnam cohort at 0.88×. Participation was 84.3% and 84.7%, both above civilians (76.8% and 77.4%).

At 10–15 years out the two cohorts are indistinguishable on unemployment: 4.5% (±0.7%) vs 4.6% (±0.9%); relative to civilians 0.95× vs 0.79×.

Right after separation (0–4 years) both cohorts show a penalty against civilians — GWOT 1.57×, Vietnam 1.14× — and the Vietnam point is not the 1973 draftee: on the separation clock it is the career soldier who left in the 1990s (mean age 49.1). Cells with an effective sample under 100 are suppressed (the GWOT cohort has not reached 23+ years out).

Disability

Across 0–22 years out, 54%–61% of GWOT in-theater veterans report a service-connected disability, against 19%–43% of Vietnam in-theater veterans at the same distance. Among those with a rating, 59%–72% of the GWOT cohort are rated 50% or higher versus 26%–33% of the Vietnam cohort (ratings asked from 1995). The disability system, not the labor market, is where the two wars differ most.

Prison

In 2004 Vietnam-era veterans were 36% of veteran state prisoners and 32% of the veteran population (CPS 2005): a representation index of 1.12. Iraq/Afghanistan-era veterans were 4% of veteran prisoners and 4.7% of veterans: 0.85. Male veterans overall were incarcerated at 630 per 100,000 against 1390 for non-veterans — a gap BJS attributes mostly to age (1253 age-controlled). The 2016 survey reports combat-veteran composition only (Vietnam 41.3%, Iraq 27.6%, Afghanistan 16.3%), so no index can be built for it.

What this cannot say

Cohort snapshots, not linked people: nobody is followed from prison to a job. Vietnam was a drafted force and GWOT an all-volunteer one, so who served differs before anything that happened to them; the only causal estimate on the Vietnam side is the draft-lottery study and there is no GWOT twin — every gap here is an association. "In-theater" is the flag the survey asks, not a campaign medal: it includes offshore and air service. The separation clock mixes short-service and career veterans at the same distance; the 0–4 bin for Vietnam is the career soldier of the 1990s. Ratings bands changed over the years; "50%+" is the one cut that is stable. Suicide and mental-health outcomes are out of scope: the VA reports them by age, not by war.
